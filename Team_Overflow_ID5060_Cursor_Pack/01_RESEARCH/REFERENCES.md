## Milk physics and chemistry
- **[R1] Muir, D. (2020). "Why does milk spill over when it boils?" New Scientist, 30 Sep 2020.** https://www.newscientist.com/article/2255722-why-does-milk-spill-over-when-it-boils/. Supports: milk has surfactants (proteins, phospholipids, glycerides, free fatty acids) that lower surface tension and stabilise froth. Denatured proteins and fat form a skin ("lactoderm") that traps foam. Stirring breaks the layer.
- **[R2] America's Test Kitchen. "Preventing Dairy from Boiling Over."** https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how-tos/5727-preventing-dairy-from-boiling-over. Supports: casein proteins gather near the surface and stabilise bubbles. A spoon or butter on the rim only *delayed* boil-over. The only sure fix was a much larger pan (a 4-quart saucepan for a pint).
- **[R3] Singh, H. (2007). Interactions of milk proteins during the manufacture of milk powders. Le Lait 87, 413–423. doi:10.1051/lait:2007014.** https://lait.dairy-journal.org/articles/lait/pdf/2007/04/l07s4513.pdf. Supports: minor whey proteins start denaturing at about 65 °C. β-lactoglobulin and α-lactalbumin denature significantly only above 70–75 °C, followed by aggregation with casein micelles.
- **[R4] Qian, F. et al. (2017). Experimental and Modelling Study of the Denaturation of Milk Protein by Heat Treatment. Korean J. Food Sci. Anim. Resour.** https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5355583/. Supports: between 70 and 100 °C whey proteins denature while casein micelle structure is not obviously changed. β-Lg binds κ-casein. Denaturation rises with temperature and time.
- **[R5] Dannenberg, F. & Kessler, H.G. (1988). Reaction kinetics of the denaturation of whey proteins in milk. J. Food Sci. 53, 258–263. doi:10.1111/j.1365-2621.1988.tb10223.x.** Supports: β-Lg denaturation has a reaction order of about 1.5 and an Arrhenius break at about 90 °C (Ea ≈ 265–280 kJ/mol below, ≈ 48–54 kJ/mol above). Use this for the course's "Arrhenius = magnification" link (slide deck 06).
- **[R6] Kamath, S., Huppertz, T., Houlihan, A.V., Deeth, H.C. (2008). The influence of temperature on the foaming of milk. Int. Dairy J. 18, 994–1002. doi:10.1016/j.idairyj.2008.05.001.** https://research.ucc.ie/en/publications/the-influence-of-temperature-on-the-foaming-of-milk/. Supports: milk foaming depends strongly on temperature (5–85 °C), fat and processing. Skim foams are most stable at 45 °C. Fat is detrimental at 15–45 °C. Homogenisation and UHT reduce the fat effect.
- **[R7] Kamath, S., Webb, R.I., Deeth, H.C. (2011). The composition of interfacial material from skim milk foams. J. Dairy Sci. doi:10.3168/jds.2010-3839.** Supports: micellar casein stabilises milk foam at higher temperatures. The casein-fraction foam half-life was about 35 min at 85 °C (lab sparged foam, not boiling).
- **[R8] Rowat lab (UCLA) (2017). Wrinkling of milk skin is mediated by evaporation. Soft Matter 13, 1056. doi:10.1039/C6SM02102F.** Supports: milk skin is a micron-thick protein–fat hydrogel at the air–water interface. Evaporation drives its stresses.
- **[R9] Goff, H.D. Dairy Science and Technology eBook, ch. 13 "Physical properties of milk", Univ. of Guelph.** https://books.lib.uoguelph.ca/dairyscienceandtechnologyebook/chapter/physical-properties-of-milk/. Supports: density 1027–1033 kg/m³ at 20 °C. Viscosity rises above 65 °C as whey proteins denature.
- **[R10] Minim, L.A. et al. (2002). Influence of temperature and water and fat contents on the thermophysical properties of milk. J. Chem. Eng. Data 47, 1488–1491. doi:10.1021/je025546a.** Supports: whole-milk specific heat 3.4–4.1 kJ/kg·K.
- **[R11] Sankar, S.R. (TN Co-op Milk Producers' Federation, Aavin), "Question Corner: Boiling milk", The Hindu, 10 Dec 2014.** https://www.thehindu.com/sci-tech/question-corner-boiling-milk/article6679872.ece. Supports: milk boils at about 100.5 °C. A cream layer traps vapour and lifts.
- **[R12] Physics StackExchange Q548716 (2020), answer by "Penguino".** https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/548716. A *hypothesis-level* source (not peer reviewed): runaway when foam production exceeds dispersal. The foam layer insulates and suppresses collapse, which gives positive feedback. We use this only as a hypothesis to test.
- **[R13] FSSAI milk standards (toned ≥3.0 % fat, ≥8.5 % SNF; double-toned ≥1.5 % fat, ≥9.0 % SNF; full cream ≥6.0 % fat, ≥9.0 % SNF).** https://fssai.gov.in/upload/advisories/2022/08/62f0a6377f7f9Direction_Operationalization_Milk_Standards_04_08_2017.pdf. Record which type we used.
- **[R14] Tetra Pak / Research Pacific India survey (2014), reported in Times of India and Business Standard.** https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/indian-moms-buy-milk-juices-without-looking-at-labels/articleshow/44944133.cms. Supports: all 845 mothers surveyed in 8 Indian cities boiled their milk, even UHT milk, typically for 4–6 min. This establishes who faces the problem and how often.

## Foam physics
- **[R15] Pugh, R.J. (2016). Bubble and Foam Chemistry, Cambridge Univ. Press, ch. 11 "Bubble size measurements and foam test methods". doi:10.1017/CBO9781316106938.012.** Supports: **Bikerman foaminess Σ = V_foam/Q = H_e/U_g (dimension: time, the average bubble lifetime).** Steady foam height scales linearly with gas flow. "The greater the quantity of solution … and the narrower the vessel, the greater the foam height." Hutzler et al. 2011 found foam height diverging at a critical gas velocity. **This is the backbone of our Π.**
- **[R16] Foam physics: Gibbs–Marangoni elasticity.** BYK e-book "What is foam?" https://www.byk.com/en/service/ebooks/defoamers-and-air-release-agents/what-is-foam and Springer reference "Foam". Supports: a pure liquid cannot make stable foam. Surfactant gradients give films elasticity.
- **[R17] Denkov, N.D. (2004). Mechanisms of foam destruction by oil-based antifoams. Langmuir 20, 9463. doi:10.1021/la049676o.** Supports: oil/fat lenses enter and bridge films and rupture them (entry coefficient E, bridging coefficient B). This is the mechanism behind "butter on the rim".

## Existing fixes and their mechanism
- **[R18] Wikipedia, "Milk watcher".** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk_watcher. Supports: the disc has a notched rim and collects steam into one large bubble that may puncture the surface film. It rattles at the boil.
- **[R19] Collections WA, "The Pot Watcher (or Milk Watcher)".** https://collectionswa.net.au/items/029480a6-90cd-467b-aa9f-58dc8e1da258. Supports: invented in 1938 in England (museum record). 8 cm glass disc.
- **[R20] Kreiner, W.A. (2015). "Der Teller in der Milch: Warum kocht sie nicht über?" Univ. Ulm OPARU. doi:10.18725/oparu-3318.** Supports: whey proteins form a skin that stops vapour escaping. Boil-over tendency is markedly lower with a porcelain milk watcher. Large disc-shaped vapour bubbles form under flat objects and break the skin. (Abstract read. The full text did not load on 21 Nov 2026.)
- **[R21] Damroo glass milk boil-over protector, Amazon.in listing B0GP78MLDF, ₹295 (MRP ₹345), seen 21 Nov 2026.** https://www.amazon.in/dp/B0GP78MLDF. Supports: an Indian disc product under ₹300. Rattles at the boil. Radius 3.65 cm. **Price changes over time: re-check before quoting.**
- **[R22] Deagon, J. & Mann, G. (2025). "Can a wooden spoon really stop a pot from boiling over?" The Conversation, 5 May 2025.** https://theconversation.com/can-a-wooden-spoon-really-stop-a-pot-from-boiling-over-heres-the-science-252519. Supports: the spoon works briefly (cool, porous, dry surface bursts bubbles) and stops working once hot and wet. Casein coats bubbles. The authors recommend a bigger pot and lower heat.
- **[R23] Kochblume silicone boil-over guard (manufacturer page).** https://www.kochblume.de/en/products/ueberkochschutz. Supports: petals lift to vent, and the bowl catches foam and drains it back. €34.95. Heat-resistant to 260 °C. Not to be used over an open flame.
- **[R24] Indian double-walled whistling milk boilers (vendor explainers).** https://rasoilab.com/cookware/milk-pan-guide/ and https://jvlclassicware.com/blogs/blog/how-milk-boiler-works. Supports: a water jacket heats the milk gently and a whistle signals readiness. Vendor claims, so treat as descriptive only.

- **[R26] WO2010129715A1, spill-over detection with a capacitive sensor around the pot base that reduces heat.** https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2010129715A1/en
- **[R27] DE3435441A1 (1984), sensor touching the rising milk surface that switches off heat or moves the pan.** https://www.freepatentsonline.com/DE3435441A1.html
- **[R28] Samsung US 12,137,837 B2 (2024), smart hood boil monitoring with camera, thermal and audio sensors.** https://exa.ai/library/legal/patent/mkpfvk8l7wp
- **[R29] MK News (Korea), 12 Mar 2026: LG DIOS "AI boiling alert", Cuckoo "AI sound detection sensor" that detects the sound of boiling over and adjusts heat, and Samsung Bespoke AI induction with a vibration-frequency sensor that reduces power.** https://www.mk.co.kr/en/business/11986312. **This strongly supports our principle: commercial hobs in 2026 use sound and vibration to beat boil-over.**
- **[R30] Bosch PerfectCook sensor (temperature sensor on the pan, boil detection on hobs).** https://e-catalog.com/BOSCH-PXY-695-DX6E.htm
- **[R31] Ondrejčák, J. & Lumnitzer, E. (2014). Sound analysis on electric kettle. Annals of Faculty Engineering Hunedoara, 2014/4.** https://annals.fih.upt.ro/pdf-full/2014/ANNALS-2014-4-01.pdf. Supports: kettle sound goes from cracking to a loud wheeze and then to smooth deep bubbling. **Maximum sound power at 60–70 °C, more than 10 dB(A) lower just before boiling (~95 °C).** Subcooled bubbles implode in cooler layers. This is our acoustic pre-boil cue (kettle, water; **milk-in-pot to be measured**).
- **[R32] nuclear-power.com, "Subcooled boiling".** https://www.nuclear-power.com/nuclear-engineering/heat-transfer/boiling-and-condensation/subcooled-boiling/. Supports: bubble collapse sound is about 100 Hz–1 kHz, which is why a kettle is noisiest before saturation.
- **[R33] MIT thesis, "Acoustic boiling detection".** http://hdl.handle.net/1721.1/28110. Supports: the subcooled boiling spectrum is continuous with a broad maximum, and spectral pattern changes with stage.
- **[R34] Tabakovski, S. et al. (2021). Boilarm, Hackster.io.** https://www.hackster.io/kuharji/boilarm-267661. Supports: a microphone-based boiling-water classifier is feasible (the authors report 97 % on their own test set, for water not milk).
- **[R35] MDN Web Docs, AnalyserNode / Web Audio API.** https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AnalyserNode. Implementation reference.
