C1. Magnify

ρliquid/ρsteam = 958/0.598 = 1602 at 100 °C (Engineering ToolBox). Net power = 1740 W × 0.68 = 1183 W (IS 4246). Water boiled = 1183/2.257×106 = 0.524 g/s. Steam = 0.524×10−3/0.598 = 0.877 L/s. Headspace of the assumed pot: π(0.14)2/4 × 0.10 − 5×10−4 = 1.04 L. If no bubble bursts, that headspace fills in 1.19 s. SOURCE → MODEL.

C2. Buckingham π

Variables He [L], Σ [T], Qv [L3/T], A [L2], hhead [L]. k = 5, r = 2, so 3 groups: π1 = He/hhead, π2 = Σ Qv/(A hhead) = Π, π3 = A/hhead2. Bikerman’s He = Σ Ug makes π1 = π2, so a steady overflow means Π > 1. With Σ = 4.6 s and the numbers in C1, Π = 4.6 × 8.77×10−4 / 1.04×10−3 = 3.88 (slides round this to 3.9). MODEL.

C3. Scale and the limit

Π at full flame / at the model’s low power: 12 cm 6.4/1.3, 14 cm 3.9/0.78, 16 cm 2.7/0.54, 20 cm 1.5/0.31, 24 cm 1.0/0.20. Closed-form time from steady steaming to the rim, t* = −τ ln(1 − 1/Π) with τ = Σ×0.85: 18 s at Π = 1.01, 1.1 s at Π = 4, and never if Π ≤ 1. The ODE is slower to the rim (2.2 s after first boil) because the skin and the vapour fraction are still rising. MODEL.

C5. Detector logic, not a milk result

On synthetic audio the state machine did three things, and only these things. A rising then fading hiss produced PRE-BOIL at 117 s and BOILING at 138 s. A scripted rattle produced BOILING. Steady noise produced no alarm. LOGIC, 3/3. The 21 s gap is a property of the made-up signal. It is not a lead time on milk. Thresholds were not searched. v1 stays at drop 4 dB, peak hold 5 s, calibration 8 s (DEFAULT_CFG in core.js). A real refinement needs one noise-only recording and one boil, then a search of dropDb ∈ {2,3,4,5} and minPeakHoldS ∈ {2,3,5} for the longest median lead with zero false alarms. That search was not run. See 03_DETECTOR_APP/REFINEMENT.md.

C7. A rim clip, drawn and not built

A clip that waits for the foam, then falls and clinks, is a late sensor. In the model the foam crosses the rim about 2.2 s after the first boil. The assumed response from the next room is 15 s, so the person loses. The milk itself does not give a useful motion either. Water density goes from about 999.8 kg/m³ at 8 °C to 958 kg/m³ at 100 °C, a swell of about 4%. On 500 mL in a 14 cm pot that is about 22 mL, or about 1.4 mm of rise. A latch that waits for expansion barely moves.

The drawn device, called Rimclip only so the file has a name, does two other things. A notched stainless disc lies in the surface for the whole heat and tries to cut the skin, which is the milk-watcher idea (Kreiner 2015). A loose head on the same pin can tap an anvil when bubbles shake that disc. The tap is meant to use the same early stage as the kettle hiss. Whether it is loud enough in the next room is untested. Whether the disc shortens Σ enough for Π < 1 is untested. The model’s disc factor of 0.45 is assumed, not fitted. Ubaal stays the selected rebuild: no new part, and in the model the cue is about 24 s before the boil. Rimclip is what we would bend if a phone were not allowed. Files: 10_RIMCLIP/rimclip.scad, rimclip.stl, rimclip_drawing.svg. Stainless only. No plastic on a gas rim.
