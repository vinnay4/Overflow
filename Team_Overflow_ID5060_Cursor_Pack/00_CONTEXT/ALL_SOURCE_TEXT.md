## 01_Project_Brief_and_Rubric
```
First Principles Thinking Term Project
Project brief and assessment rubric with a worked example
Submission deadline 25 Nov. 2026
Course weight 20 percent | Assessment 100 marks

The task
Choose one everyday product, process or service, or a persistent problem. Investigate how it works, question the
assumptions behind the current solution, identify the governing first principles, and rebuild it to achieve a useful
improvement. Build or pilot your proposed change and evaluate it against the current approach.
Your improvement may reduce cost, completion time, effort, material use, energy use or errors. It may improve
reliability, accessibility or user experience. Choose one primary outcome and identify other outcomes that must
remain acceptable.

Choose a manageable scope
Select something you can observe, investigate and test before the deadline. A modest improvement supported by
sound reasoning and evidence is a suitable project. Define the essential function before committing to a particular
solution.

Type              Possible choices

Product           A pen, toothbrush, paper clip, storage container, packaging or simple kitchen device.

Process           Library lending, course feedback, food preparation, lab booking or a repeated administrative task.

Service           Campus transport, food delivery, queue management or information access.

Persistent
                  Repeated waiting, misplaced objects, confusing instructions, avoidable waste or recurring errors.
problem

What you should demonstrate
Show a traceable connection from evidence to principles, from principles to a design decision, and from the
design decision to a test. Explain what you changed, why it should work, what actually happened and what you
would improve next.
A product may use a simple physical prototype. A process or service may use a small pilot, an enacted workflow or
a functional mock-up tested with users. Choose a form that tests the central mechanism of your improvement.
How to carry out the project
1 Define the problem and establish the baseline
State who experiences the problem, where it occurs and what outcome matters. Record the current performance
using observation, measurements or user accounts. Set a measurable target and list constraints such as budget,
safety, space and available resources.

2 Gather information and deconstruct
Observe the actual product, process or service. Break it into components, actions, flows and interactions. Use
photographs, an exploded view, a process map or a system boundary diagram. Record where time, effort,
material, information or energy is used or lost.
Use questioning to investigate causes. A Five Whys sequence should follow a connected causal chain; check each
answer against evidence. Record relevant changes in the design or process over time and explain which
constraints may have shaped them. Label unsupported historical explanations as hypotheses.

3 Separate assumptions from first principles
Make three categories explicit: observations you have recorded; assumptions or hypotheses that need checking;
and governing principles or essential constraints. A familiar practice is not automatically a fundamental truth. For
services, examine capacity, conservation of tasks or resources, dependencies and information requirements. Test
claims about user behaviour locally.

4 Apply first principles transformers
Use at least two relevant transformers. For each, state what you transformed, the mathematical operation it
suggests, the insight it revealed and how that insight changed your rebuilding. Select transformers for their
usefulness rather than using all of them.
Possible transformers include Magnify, Scale, Approximate, Idealize, Normalize, Interpolate, Extrapolate,
Aggregate, Decompose, Linearize, Change Frame and Perturb. Other choices include Shift, Rotate, Superpose,
Accumulate, Project, Iterate and Approach a Limit.

5 Rebuild and choose a solution
Generate at least three meaningfully different alternatives from your principles. Compare them against the
primary target, constraints and trade-offs. Choose one and explain the mechanism by which it should improve the
outcome. Include an annotated sketch or a revised process or service map.

6 Implement test and refine
Build or pilot the selected solution. Compare it with the baseline under similar conditions. Repeat measurements
where feasible, record variation and obtain relevant user feedback. Make at least one refinement, or explain with
evidence why the first version should be retained. State limitations and distinguish measured improvements from
estimates.
Submission and suggested milestones
Submit a presentation and evidence
Submit a presentation of no more than six main slides, together with a concise evidence appendix of up to four
pages. A short demonstration video of up to two minutes may be included when it helps show the
implementation. Put contributor names and roll numbers on the first slide and identify each person’s contribution
in the appendix where applicable. Make sure to take enough photos and videos so that you can appropriately
choose what to show in your report.

Slide     Required content

     1    Chosen product, process, service or problem; photograph or diagram; user need; baseline and target.

     2    Deconstruction; field evidence; relevant evolution of the current approach; assumptions questioned.

     3    Governing first principles; at least two transformer applications and the insights they produce.

     4    At least three alternatives; comparison; selected reconstruction and an annotated sketch or workflow.

     5    What was built or piloted; test method; before and after evidence; refinement.

     6    Measured benefits; cost and trade-offs; limitations; next step; key references.

Use the appendix for raw observations, trial data, calculations, additional references, prototype photographs and
contributions. The appendix supports the six slides; it should not become a second presentation.

Suggested progress schedule

By        Suggested progress

10 Oct.   Choose the problem, observe the current situation and define scope and report in GC (5 marks out of 20)

24 Oct.   Complete baseline evidence, deconstruction and first principles analysis.

4 Nov.    Apply transformers, compare alternatives and build or pilot the first version.

20 Nov. Complete comparison tests and make a refinement.

25 Nov. Submit the six slides and evidence appendix. (15 marks out of 20)

Evidence and attribution
Identify the source of each important factual claim. If you use AI or external assistance, state what it helped with
and verify the claims and calculations you retain. Observations, measurements and implementation records must
represent work actually carried out. Obtain permission for access or trials where needed and protect participants’
privacy.
An honest unsuccessful trial can earn strong marks when the reasoning, implementation, testing and learning are
sound. A large claimed gain without supporting evidence will receive limited credit for validation.

Mathematical Relationship:

Why First Principles Matter: The gear ratio principle

• multiplicative scaling can solve complex problems, like

                                   Hydraulic Systems


Phenomenon: A small force applied to a narrow piston
         magnifies into a much larger force on a wider piston.
Mathematical Relationship:
• Pascal's principle (pressure is transmitted equally in fluids)
• Magnification: F_large = F_small * (A_large / A_small),
• pressure P = F / A being constant.
Why First Principles Matter: A_large / A_small
• led to innovations like heavy machinery lifts
• blood pressure scales in veins of different sizes.
• Use this for scalable designs for robotics or medical devices.
                       Wheatstone Bridge


Phenomenon: detects tiny changes in resistance by balancing a circuit. A
   small imbalance creates a measurable voltage, magnifying subtle
   variations into readable signals
Mathematical Relationship:
• R1/R2 = R3/Rx (ratio of resistances)  Kirchhoff's laws.
• For δRx, output ΔV ≈ (Vin*δRx)/(4 Rx) linearly amplifies change.
• V_out = V_in * [(R3/(R3+Rx)) - (R2/(R1+R2))], zero at balance.
Why First Principles Matter: Circuit theory basics (null method)
• precise sensors, in bridges for struct monitoring or lab equips.
• It shows how ratio-based null methods scale accuracy, inspiring modern
  analogs like operational amplifiers in electronics or even quantum sensors.

Mathematical Relationship:
• Linear magnification m = himage / hobject = -v / u, from the lens formula 1/f =
