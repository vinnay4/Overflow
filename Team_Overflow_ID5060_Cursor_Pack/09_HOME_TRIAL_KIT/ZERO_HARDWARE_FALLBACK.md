# If you truly cannot run milk trials before 25 Nov: the honest minimum

Ranked by value per minute. Do as many as you can, and **say exactly what was and wasn't done**.

1. **(20 min, strongly recommended) 2 baseline milk boils + 1 Ubaal milk boil, 250 mL each.** Even n = 3 real trials beats a perfect simulation with n = 0. The deck becomes "pilot evidence, n = 3".
2. **(15 min) Water trials with Ubaal** if milk is not possible: they test the acoustic cue (the central mechanism of the app) on the physics the literature describes. Report them as "water, not milk" (a limitation).
3. **(10 min) Human response-time test** (no stove): a real number for the latency budget.
4. **(10 min) Use existing videos?** Only with permission and attribution. Public milk boil-over videos may be used to *illustrate*, never as "our observation". You can scrub one for a foam-rise curve **labelled as third-party data** with its URL. Prefer your own.
5. **(0 min) Functional mock-up pilot** (the brief allows "a functional mock-up tested with users" for process/service changes): play a kitchen recording into Ubaal and have 3 people respond to the alarm. This measures response time and usability (SUS-style 1–5 questions). Report it as a pilot of the information loop, not of boil-over prevention.

**What to write on slide 5/6 if there are no milk trials:** "We could not run unattended milk trials before submission (placement season). Our evidence is: (a) synthetic tests of the detector logic, (b) __ water trials, (c) a calibrated model with clearly labelled estimates, (d) human response-time measurements. The primary outcome (boil-overs in 10 unattended trials) remains **untested**." The rubric rewards honesty. It penalises claimed gains without evidence.
